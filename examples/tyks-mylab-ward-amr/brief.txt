Antibiotic resistance on the ward: spot it early

Tyks Laboratories and Mylab

Antibiotic resistance is one of the biggest threats to hospital care. Every day the clinical microbiology laboratory produces hundreds of culture results with susceptibility tests: which bacteria grew from which sample, on which ward, and which antibiotics they were resistant to. Today this information is summarised in a hospital-wide antibiogram once a year. Clinicians on a ward cannot see that, for example, ESBL-producing E. coli in urine samples from one surgical ward has been rising over the last six weeks.

Your challenge is to build a rolling, ward-level antibiogram and a resistance trend detection tool for infection control nurses and antimicrobial stewardship teams. The tool should show, per ward and per bacterium-antibiotic combination, the current share of resistant isolates, how it compares with the ward's own history and with the rest of the hospital, and raise an alert when a resistance trend or a cluster of cases looks unusual. Results should be shown in a form clinicians can act on during the ward round: which empirical antibiotic is likely to work on this ward today.

Partner data: anonymised culture and susceptibility results (species, specimen type, ward, date, antibiotic, S/I/R interpretation), with small numbers per ward and per month. Expect missing values, duplicate isolates from the same patient and changes in testing standards over time.

Think about statistical reliability with small samples, alert fatigue, and how to explain why an alert fired.
